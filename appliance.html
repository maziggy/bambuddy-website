Appliance

Self-hosting, without the hosting.

Bambuddy on a Raspberry Pi, as an appliance rather than a project. You write one image to a memory card and the box does the rest — no Docker to learn, no compose file to edit, no cloud account to create. Your prints, your data, your network.

An annual subscription, from October 2026. You supply the Raspberry Pi; we supply the image, every update to it, and someone to ask.

You keep your own hardware Nothing to ship, nothing to wait for Bambuddy itself stays free

What it does for you

Fifteen minutes from card to first print

Flash the image, plug in ethernet or join the appliance's own WiFi network, and a setup wizard opens in your browser. No screen, no keyboard.

Upgrades that undo themselves

A new Bambuddy version is pulled, started, and health-checked. If it doesn't come up, the appliance puts the old one back on its own.

Two copies of its own operating system

System updates are written to the spare copy and booted once to prove they work. A bad one falls back to the copy that was fine, by itself.

Its own admin panel

Temperature, power flags, disk, logs, updates and network — on a separate port, so you can still fix the box when Bambuddy itself is down.

Reach it from anywhere

Sign in to Tailscale from the panel and reach the appliance and its virtual printers remotely. No router ports opened, no cloud in the middle.

A way back from every state

A factory reset from the admin panel, or over SSH when the panel is unreachable, returns you to a fresh setup wizard.

What you need to buy

Four parts from any Raspberry Pi dealer, plus a case you print yourself. Roughly €120–150 depending on where you shop, and it is yours — not rented from us.

Raspberry Pi 5, 4 GB

8 GB if you run a large fleet. A Pi 4 will not work: the two-copy update layout needs the Pi 5's bootloader.

The official 27 W supply

Undervoltage is the most common cause of a mysteriously unreliable Pi, and it shows up as dropped printer connections rather than an error.

The official Active Cooler

This box runs every hour of every day. A throttled Pi looks exactly like slow software.

microSD, 64 GB or more

Endurance-rated if you can. The two system copies claim 21 GB before any of your data, and the appliance checks the card on every boot.

A case you print

Download the 3MF and print it. The Pi is held in it by 4 × M2.5 × 5 screws and 4 × M2.5 nuts — the only parts not on the list above.

Four things worth saying plainly

The tier is about how you use it, not how many printers you have. There is no metering, no seat count and nothing to police. A hobbyist with twelve printers is Personal; a shop with two is Business.

If you stop paying, your appliance keeps working. Exactly as it is, indefinitely. What stops is updates and support — not the software you already have. Nothing is held hostage.

Per appliance, not per printer. One subscription covers one box, however many printers you point it at.

Business is support for the appliance, not for your print floor. It covers the box: the image, updates, registration, and faults in the appliance itself. It is not a support contract for running a fleet — not your network, not your printers, and not Bambuddy as an application. If that is what you need, it is a different conversation with a contract of its own, and €249 a year is not it.

What support actually means

One person answers it. Better to be exact about that now than to let you find out later.

By email, to one address, whichever tier you are on. Business requests are handled first, and that follows from your subscription rather than from a second inbox you have to remember. Monday to Friday, 09:00 to 17:00 Central European Time — which covers most of the morning on the US east coast, and almost none of the working day on the west.

Business response time: normally the next working day, within two at the latest. What is promised is a response, not a fix. Nobody can honestly promise a fix by a date, and a promise is only worth what it is worth in the worst week of the year.

For up to fifteen working days a year, that clock stops. Holiday, illness, the ordinary reasons. Planned absences are announced a week ahead, by email and in the admin panel. One person cannot cover a whole year without them, and a promise that pretends otherwise is one that breaks when it matters.

What it does not cover: your network, your printers and their firmware, print results, hardware outside the list above, and your own containers or modifications. Nor Bambuddy itself as an application — bugs there belong in the public issue tracker, where they are handled the same for everyone, subscription or not.

Already running Bambuddy somewhere else?

Moving an existing installation — Docker, or a server of your own — onto the appliance is a fixed €149, once. It is a separate job rather than part of the subscription, and it covers your data and your printer connections: not rebuilding containers, automations or system changes you have built around it.

It is still the same Bambuddy

The appliance runs the ordinary, open-source, AGPL-licensed Bambuddy — the same container you would install yourself. There are no exclusive features and no functionality locked behind the subscription. There never will be.

What you are paying for is everything around it: an image that is already configured, updates that are tested together and undo themselves when they go wrong, an admin panel for the box itself, and a person to ask when something is strange. If you would rather do all of that yourself, the installation guide has everything you need, costs nothing, and always will.

What it sends home

Five things, once on first boot and then about once a day: an identifier for the board, the batch the image came from, your subscription key, the hardware model, and the appliance version. In between, once an hour, it asks one short question — is this subscription still valid? — and sends nothing but its identifier and the token it was given. That check writes nothing on our side; it exists so a renewal or a cancellation reaches the box within the hour instead of a day later.

The identifier is derived from the Raspberry Pi's own serial number rather than generated at random, so it survives rewriting the card — that is what lets a subscription follow the box through a reinstall instead of being spent twice. Your IP address is stored only as a salted hash, never in the clear, and only to notice one key running on several boards at once.

No printer data. No print history. No files. No user accounts. No credentials. Nothing about what you print, when, or with what. That is how a subscription is matched to a box and offered updates — and if it never reaches us, the appliance works exactly the same.
